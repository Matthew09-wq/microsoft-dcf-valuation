# Microsoft DCF Valuation

## Overview

This project presents a 10-year discounted cash flow valuation of Microsoft Corporation (NASDAQ: MSFT), developed as part of my financial analysis portfolio.

The analysis examines Microsoft's historical financial performance, elevated investment in AI and cloud infrastructure, long-term capital intensity, free cash flow generation, and the expectations embedded in its market valuation.

## Key Valuation Outputs

- **Reference Market Price:** $493.95
- **Base Case DCF Value:** $354.42 per share
- **Bear Case:** $242.44 per share
- **Bull Case:** $455.76 per share
- **WACC:** 9.34%
- **Terminal Growth Rate:** 2.50%
- **Market-Implied Terminal Growth:** 5.12%
- **Market-Implied WACC:** 7.53%

The difference between the DCF value and the reference market price is treated as a measure of the assumptions embedded in the prevailing valuation, rather than evidence that Microsoft is necessarily mispriced.

## Analysis Includes

- FY2022 to FY2026 historical financial analysis
- 10-year operating forecast from FY2027E to FY2036E
- Unlevered free cash flow valuation
- PP&E depreciation and capital expenditure modelling
- Bear, Base and Bull scenario analysis
- WACC and terminal growth sensitivity analysis
- Reverse DCF analysis
- Terminal-year steady-state testing

## Methodology

Unlevered free cash flow is calculated as:

**NOPAT + Depreciation & Amortisation - Capital Expenditure + Working Capital Cash Impact**

The valuation uses a 10-year explicit forecast with mid-year discounting, followed by a Gordon Growth terminal value.

PP&E depreciation is modelled separately from intangible amortisation, allowing the PP&E roll-forward and capital expenditure assumptions to be evaluated independently.

The terminal year is calibrated to satisfy a steady-state relationship between capital expenditure, depreciation, long-term growth and the opening PP&E balance.

Scenario analysis is used to assess alternative operating outcomes, while sensitivity analysis evaluates the impact of different WACC and terminal growth assumptions. A reverse DCF is also used to estimate the assumptions required to reconcile the model with the reference market price.

## Investment Question

Microsoft has generated strong revenue and operating income growth while significantly increasing investment in AI and cloud infrastructure.

The central question explored in this analysis is whether the cash returns generated from this elevated investment programme will be sufficient to justify the expectations embedded in Microsoft's market valuation.

Under the Base case, the model produces an implied value of **$354.42 per share** compared with a reference market price of **$493.95**.

Rather than interpreting this difference as definitive evidence of mispricing, the analysis focuses on the growth, capital efficiency and required-return assumptions necessary to support the prevailing market valuation.
